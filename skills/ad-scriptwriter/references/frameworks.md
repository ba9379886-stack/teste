## 1. PAS — Problem → Agitate → Solution

**Best for:** Pain-point products, SaaS, direct response, dental, wellness, med spa, AI tools
**Funnel stage:** MOFU (Middle of Funnel — problem-aware audience)
**Sweet spot length:** 30–40 seconds

**Structure:**
- **Problem (0–5s):** Name the pain clearly. Who has it, what it feels like.
- **Agitate (5–15s):** Twist the knife. What does this pain COST them? What happens if they don't fix it? What have they already tried that failed?
- **Solution (15–30s):** Present your product/service as the inevitable answer. Don't over-explain HOW — just what it delivers.

**Example (35s SaaS ad):**
> "Still spending 4 hours a day chasing cold leads that never respond? Meanwhile your competitors are booking calls on autopilot. Every hour you waste manually prospecting is revenue walking out the door. [Product] finds, qualifies, and books meetings for you — completely automated. 300+ agencies switched last quarter. Start your free trial — link below."

**Why it works:** Directly activates loss aversion. Agitation phase amplifies existing pain, making the solution feel like relief, not a pitch.

## 2. Hook-Story-Offer (Russell Brunson)

**Best for:** Coaching, info products, founder-led ads, high-ticket services, DTC brands
**Funnel stage:** TOFU–MOFU
**Sweet spot length:** 40–50 seconds

**Structure:**
- **Hook (0–5s):** Curiosity-driven opening. Open a loop the viewer must close.
- **Story (5–35s):** Compressed narrative. Personal or customer story that breaks a "false belief." Include: failed attempts → rock bottom → discovery → transformation. Get vulnerable — emotions, not just facts.
- **Offer (35–50s):** Present the mechanism. Don't reveal HOW — introduce it as "the thing that changed everything." Then state the offer and CTA.

**Key insight (Brunson):** "If something isn't working, it's ALWAYS the hook, the story, or the offer." Diagnose failure at this level.

**Story formula:**
Failed attempt 1 → Failed attempt 2 → Rock bottom moment (emotional) → Discovery of new mechanism → Transformation → "That's why I created [offer]"

**Why it works:** Story bypasses the logical skepticism filter. People don't argue with a narrative the way they argue with claims. The false belief reframe (the "truth bomb") makes the solution feel inevitable.

## 3. BAB — Before → After → Bridge

**Best for:** Transformation products, aesthetics, med spa, dental, weight loss, fitness, coaching
**Funnel stage:** TOFU
**Sweet spot length:** 30–40 seconds

**Structure:**
- **Before (0–8s):** Paint the undesirable current state vividly. Make the viewer feel this is exactly them.
- **After (8–20s):** Show the aspirational future state. Specific, visual, desirable. Not vague — concrete outcomes.
- **Bridge (20–40s):** Position your product/service as the bridge that gets them from Before to After. Include one proof element.

**Works especially well with:** Before/after visual content (skin transformation, smile makeover, body sculpting). The visual IS the ad — the script reinforces it.

**Compliance note:** In weight loss and aesthetics — NEVER show before/after side-by-side. Show "after" in hook, "before" later in video (minimum 5 seconds apart). Use "went from X to Y" not "lost X lbs in Y weeks."

## 4. AIDA — Attention → Interest → Desire → Action

**Best for:** Aspirational products, awareness-stage campaigns, premium services
**Funnel stage:** TOFU
**Sweet spot length:** 40–50 seconds (needs full arc)

**Structure:**
- **Attention (0–3s):** Hook — stop the scroll
- **Interest (3–10s):** Introduce a compelling benefit or provocative idea that earns the next 30 seconds
- **Desire (10–38s):** Build desire through outcomes, transformation stories, aspirational imagery, social proof
- **Action (38–50s):** Clear, urgent CTA

**Why it works:** Full emotional arc. Builds desire before asking for action — essential when targeting people who don't yet know they want what you're selling.

## 5. 4Ps — Problem → Promise → Proof → Push

**Best for:** Products with strong social proof, retargeting, bottom-funnel
**Funnel stage:** BOFU (Bottom of Funnel)
**Sweet spot length:** 40–50 seconds

**Structure:**
- **Problem (0–8s):** Identify the pain — briefly, the viewer already knows it
- **Promise (8–20s):** State exactly what your solution delivers. Specific, measurable outcome.
- **Proof (20–38s):** Evidence. Testimonials, specific client results, numbers, case studies.
- **Push (38–50s):** Urgency + CTA. Why act NOW rather than later?

**Best combined with:** Risk reversal (money-back guarantee, "try for free"). Retargeting audiences who already visited the site or watched previous ads.

## 6. PASTOR

**Best for:** High-ticket coaching, consulting, premium services requiring deep emotional connection
**Funnel stage:** MOFU–BOFU
**Sweet spot length:** 45–50 seconds (can extend to 90s for extremely cold traffic)

**Structure:**
- **Problem (0–5s):** Name the problem deeply — what is it costing them?
- **Amplify (5–12s):** Amplify the pain. What's the worst-case if nothing changes?
- **Story (12–25s):** Customer story or your own story. Specific journey.
- **Transformation (25–35s):** The shift. What changed and why.
- **Offer (35–42s):** Present the mechanism and package.
- **Response (42–50s):** Clear CTA with urgency or scarcity.

**Why it works:** The amplification phase is unique — it makes inaction feel dangerous, which is a stronger motivator than desire for gain. Especially effective in health, financial, and career transformation niches.

## 7. Indirect Ad Formula (Lead Magnet / Free Resource)

**Best for:** B2C coaches, consultants, building email list, brand trust, high-ticket funnels
**When NOT to use:** When your offer is "done-for-you" (teaching them how to do it themselves = incongruent)
**Funnel stage:** TOFU — audience doesn't know you yet

**Formula:** Hook → Story → Offer (lead magnet) → First CTA → Proof → What You'll Learn → Final CTA

**Key principles:**
- You are NOT selling your core offer. You are selling the first step in the funnel.
- Hook must call out the problem + who it's for
- Story: vulnerable, emotional, specific — failed attempts + rock bottom + discovery
- Offer: introduce as NEW MECHANISM. Don't reveal HOW — just that it changed everything.
- First CTA: don't wait until the end — give them a chance to click early
- Proof: 1–2 named case studies with specific results AFTER the first CTA
- Final CTA: straight to camera, mild urgency optional

**Avatar mismatch strategy:** If you're a male coach working with women — have a female client film the story-based ad, then she plugs you as the expert who helped her. You get avatar connection AND authority positioning.

**Why indirect > direct for cold traffic:**
- Higher show rates on sales calls (people arrive knowing, liking, trusting you)
- Higher close rates (value has been exchanged)
- Builds list, community, YouTube, brand simultaneously

## 8. Direct Ad Formula

**Works ONLY when at least ONE of these is true:**
1. You have 50,000+ followers or established brand recognition
2. You serve an extremely specific niche (ultramarathon athletes, pickleball players)
3. Your audience has extremely high pain (knee pain, back pain, IBS, bloating)
4. You have a strong risk reversal ("If it doesn't work, I'll pay YOU $1,000")

**Without one of these four:** direct ads produce cheap leads who don't show, don't buy, don't trust you.

**Short version (30–40s):** Hook → Promise + Risk Reversal → Proof → CTA

**Full version (2–3 minutes):**
Hook → Promise + Risk Reversal → Qualify → Proof → First CTA → Introduce Yourself → Your Story → Your Method → More Proof → Restate Risk Reversal → Final CTA

**Critical:** You pay the same CPM whether the ad is 30 seconds or 3 minutes. If someone is still watching at 90 seconds, sell them harder. Cold audiences need more trust-building time.

**Hook rule for direct ads:** ALWAYS call out avatar explicitly by demographics AND problem in the first sentence. Never rely on appearance alone.
> "Ladies over 50 — if you're struggling to lose weight and don't feel as confident as you used to, watch this video."

## 9. The Second-by-Second 60s Framework

The brain asks every 2–3 seconds: "Why should I keep watching?"
The ad must rotate through: **Pattern Interrupt → Pain → Authority → Proof → Clarity → Urgency**

| Seconds | Lever | Goal | Visuals |
|---------|-------|------|---------|
| 0–3s | Pattern Interrupt | Stop the scroll | Face cam, direct eye contact, big subtitles |
| 3–6s | Pain Expansion | "This is happening to me" | Quick cuts, stressed expressions, problem B-roll |
| 6–10s | Authority Signal | Why trust you? | State credentials with relevance to THEIR problem |
| 10–15s | Truth Bomb | "Aha moment" — reframe false belief | Straight to camera, confident |
| 15–25s | Solution Introduction | Product as natural answer | Introduce AFTER the reframe — never before |
| 25–35s | Learning Outcomes | Clarity on what they get | Bullet points on screen |
| 35–42s | Social Proof | Reduce risk | Testimonials, DM screenshots, comments |
| 42–50s | Audience Filter | Who it IS and IS NOT for | Disqualification builds trust |
| 50–57s | Urgency + Offer | Act NOW | Limited seats, countdown, bonus |
| 57–60s | CTA | One clear action | Arrow animations, button highlight |

**NEVER** start with "Hi guys" or a self-introduction in the first 3 seconds.
The only appropriate moment for brief self-introduction is the Authority Signal (6–10s).

## 10. UGC 5-Step Backbone

_Added: 2026-04 | Source: UGC ad industry analysis_

**Best for:** UGC-style ads, creator content, customer testimonial-style ads, authentic talking-to-camera
**Funnel stage:** TOFU–MOFU
**Length:** 30–60 seconds

The variation between high-performing UGC ads is HOW each step is expressed (POV story,
testimonial, GRWM, demo) — not the steps themselves. Always fill all 5 slots.

**Structure:**

### Step 1 — Hook (0–3s)
Stop the scroll. Filter to the right avatar. Pattern interrupt.
- The visual hook has more performance impact than the verbal hook
- Plan the physical action on screen, not just the words
- See hooks.md for full library

### Step 2 — Problem + Agitation (3–12s)
Describe the pain in **specific, scene-based terms** — not abstract statements.

✅ Strong: "I kept refilming my videos because my audio sounded like I was recording in a tunnel"
❌ Weak: "My audio quality was poor"

Agitation = 2–4 short lines that deepen the frustration or consequences (lost time, wasted money,
missed outcome, ruined results). Stay sharp — don't wallow. Switch to solution quickly.

**Language rule:** Mine real customer reviews, Reddit threads, and DMs for this language. Use the
exact words customers use to describe the problem — not your internal description of it.

### Step 3 — Product Enters as Discovery, Not a Pitch (12–22s)
The product or service enters as a **personal discovery or recommendation**, never as a formal pitch.

Natural framing:
- "A friend recommended it after seeing me struggle with [X]"
- "I saw it in someone else's video and had to try it"
- "I'd tried [alternative] and it didn't work — then someone told me about [product]"
- "Everyone in the comments kept asking what I was using — it was [product]"

The first product line often embeds **subtle proof** naturally:
- "My friend swore by it" → social proof without a hard claim
- "Everyone in the comments asked me about it" → implied popularity

### Step 4 — Benefits and Transformation (22–38s)
Translate every feature into a **concrete visual transformation**.

✅ Strong: "I've washed these sheets half as often" / "I stopped refilming videos"
❌ Weak: "Silver-infused antimicrobial" / "Dual-channel noise cancellation"

Deliver 1–3 transformations. Keep them practical and specific.
The identity shift matters: not just what changed, but how their life/work/confidence changed.

### Step 5 — Social Proof + CTA (38–60s)
Proof should be **specific and low-drama** — not over-the-top. Options:
- Casual mentions of others using it (partner, friends, coworkers)
- A single believable superlative: "First [product category] I've ever repurchased"
- Quick review overlays or before/after shots (≤1 second each) — let visuals do the work
- In very visual concepts (makeup, before/after), the story itself IS the proof — stay minimal

CTA: One clear action. Urgency if applicable.

**Why UGC works:** It bypasses ad skepticism. It reads like a friend's recommendation, not a
brand pitch. "FaceTime, not brand deck."

## 11. Mini VSL — Supplement / Ecom (7-Step)

_Added: 2026-04 | Source: Mini VSL industry analysis + Meta supplement campaign research_

**Best for:** Supplements, health products, fitness, high-ROI ecom — anything that scales
with larger budgets and benefits from logical persuasion
**Funnel stage:** TOFU–MOFU cold traffic
**Length:** 2–5 minutes
**Format:** Vertical or square, mobile-first. Short, subtitle-friendly lines throughout.
**Editing assumption:** Cut every 3–5 seconds. Write lines to match that rhythm.

Mini VSLs are compressed long-form. Every line must be a **micro-hook** — a small reason
to keep watching one more sentence. Write it like a series of curiosity gaps.

**The 7-Step Structure:**

### Step 1 — Hook (Written Last, Placed First)
Must feel like a strong TikTok/Reels opening — **both visual AND verbal pattern interrupt**.
- Strong visual action on screen + spoken line that creates immediate curiosity or identifies pain
- Do NOT reveal the mechanism or product yet — open a loop the viewer must close
- Example: "I tried everything for [problem] for 3 years — until I found the one thing my doctor
  never mentioned."

### Step 2 — Intro + Emotional Appeal (Re-hook after 3s)
2–4 short lines naming the core pain and specific symptoms.
Design this section to **re-hook attention** — you've kept them past 3 seconds, now lock them in.
Name the exact daily experience: "You wake up tired even after 8 hours. You can't focus.
You've tried [supplement/product/diet] and felt nothing."

### Step 3 — Problem + Unique Mechanism of the Problem
Cover what they've already tried that failed → then reveal a **new underlying cause** that
explains all the failures.

This is the "aha" section that makes everything click:
"The reason [previous solution] didn't work for you is that it was targeting [wrong thing].
The REAL cause is [new mechanism]."

Keep the language simple and memorable. Avoid scientific jargon — use plain analogies.

### Step 4 — Unique Mechanism of the Solution
The logical bridge: if X is the real cause, then Y is the real fix.
Name the mechanism in simple, memorable language — one phrase the viewer can hold in their mind.
"That's why we focus on [mechanism name] instead of [what others do]."

This is the intellectual core of the VSL. It's what makes your product make sense over alternatives.

### Step 5 — Product + How It Works
One line linking the mechanism to the product. Then one simple usage explanation.
- When to take it / how to use it / what to do
- Keep this short — they already understand WHY, now you're just answering HOW
- One key differentiator or proprietary ingredient/process

### Step 6 — Social Proof
Fast montage of testimonials, UGC clips, or screenshots.
- Does not hijack the main narrative — keep it moving
- Use specific transformations, not vague praise ("I lost 14 lbs" beats "I feel amazing")
- Mix formats: video testimonials + text screenshots + before/after visuals
- In health/supplements: doctor endorsements or clinical reference adds credibility

### Step 7 — Urgency + CTA
Scarcity, discount, or guarantee + one clear instruction.
- Make the urgency REAL — limited batch, price increase, time-limited offer
- Include risk reversal (money-back guarantee) to remove final hesitation
- One action only: "Click the button below," "Tap the link," "Order now"
- Restate the transformation promise in the CTA: "Click below and start [result] in [timeframe]"

**Key principle:** The script is responsible for attracting the right person AND repelling the
wrong one. Broad targeting + strong creative = creative-as-targeting. Write for one specific
avatar at one specific awareness level.

## 12. Mini VSL — Lead-Gen / Service (8-Step)

_Added: 2026-04 | Source: Agency/coaching VSL analysis + service business ad research_

**Best for:** Agencies, coaching, consulting, service businesses — any offer where the next
step is a call, application, or demo
**Funnel stage:** TOFU–MOFU cold traffic
**Length:** 3–5 minutes
**Key difference from Supplement VSL:** Lead with BUSINESS OUTCOME, not mechanism.
Showing the booking process inside the video dramatically increases follow-through.

**The 8-Step Structure:**

### Step 1 — Big Promise / Hook
Focused entirely on transformation outcome, not mechanism.
Lead with a business result or life outcome the avatar desperately wants.
"How [type of client] went from [before state] to [specific result] in [timeframe] — without [big objection]."

Do NOT lead with "we run Meta ads" or "our agency does X." Lead with what THEY get.

### Step 2 — Immediate Social Proof (Mini Case Studies)
2–3 rapid-fire specific results from real clients.
Name + outcome + timeframe if possible.
"Sarah: went from 2 booked calls a week to 11. Mark: first $30K month after 6 weeks. Jenny: quit
her job 90 days in."

These don't need to be long — one line each, fast. The goal is to establish "this is real and
it works for people like me."

### Step 3 — Twist the Knife (Before vs. After Contrast)
Paint the before state in visceral, specific detail — the frustration, the wasted money,
the failed attempts, the exhaustion.
Make the viewer feel it. Then contrast with the after state.
This emotional gap is what makes the solution feel worth paying for.

### Step 4 — Introduce the Solution (3 Pillars — No Full How-To)
Name your method, system, or approach. Break it into 3 clear components/pillars.
**Do NOT give away the full implementation.** Enough for them to understand the logic,
not enough to DIY it. This creates demand for the next step (the call/application).

"The [Method Name] has three parts: [Pillar 1], [Pillar 2], [Pillar 3]. Here's why each one
matters... [brief explanation of each — the what, not the how]."

### Step 5 — "Imagine This" — Future Pacing
Help them picture their life/business AFTER working with you.
Specific, sensory, emotional. "Imagine waking up Monday with 4 new calls already booked.
Imagine not having to chase leads. Imagine [specific daily reality they want]."

Future pacing creates desire so strong the CTA feels like a logical next step, not a push.

### Step 6 — First CTA + Show the Next Step
Don't wait until the end for the first CTA — give a click opportunity here.
**Then show the booking or application process on screen** (screen recording or step-by-step).
Walk through exactly what happens: "You'll click the link, fill out a 2-minute form, then
you'll get a calendar link to pick your time." Removing mystery increases follow-through.

### Step 7 — Second CTA + Reinforcement
Restate the transformation promise. Address the final objection (usually price, time, or doubt).
Add risk reversal if applicable.
Restate the specific action: click, apply, book.

### Step 8 — Outro with Visual CTA
Text on screen with the offer and CTA. Clean ending.
Optional: brief "who this is NOT for" disqualifier — builds trust with the right people by
showing you're not for everyone.

## 13. Framework Selection Guide

**Quick-decision logic:**

```
Is the audience cold (never heard of you)?
  → Use Indirect Formula, Hook-Story-Offer, BAB, or AIDA
  → For UGC format: use UGC 5-Step Backbone (Framework 10)
  → For Mini VSL (supplements/ecom): use Framework 11
  → For Mini VSL (agency/coaching/service): use Framework 12

Is the audience warm (visited site, engaged with content)?
  → Use PAS, Hook-Story-Offer, or PASTOR

Is the audience hot (retargeting — watched video, abandoned form)?
  → Use 4Ps or Direct Ad Formula with strong risk reversal

Is the offer a transformation (weight loss, aesthetics, smile, fitness)?
  → Short form: Lead with BAB
  → Long form: Mini VSL Supplement (Framework 11) — unique mechanism is key

Is the offer high-ticket ($1,000+)?
  → Use PASTOR or Indirect Formula + pre-call VSL
  → Lead-gen VSL (Framework 12) for agency/coaching

Is the ad type UGC?
  → Always use UGC 5-Step Backbone (Framework 10)

Is the ad type Mini VSL?
  → Supplements, health, ecom → Framework 11 (7-step)
  → Agency, coaching, consulting, service → Framework 12 (8-step)

Is there strong social proof available?
  → Lead with 4Ps or proof-first hook on any framework

Is the ad for an AI/tech product?
  → Use PAS or Hook-Story-Offer, lead with outcome not technology

Is the ad for a dealership?
  → Direct Ad Formula, lead with payment ("$399/month") not price

What is the buyer's awareness level?
  → Unaware: Framework 11/12 (Mini VSL educates) or BAB (shows transformation before naming cause)
  → Problem-aware: PAS, PASTOR — lean into agitation heavily
  → Solution-aware: Hook-Story-Offer with unique mechanism angle; bold comparison hook
  → Brand-aware: 4Ps or Direct Ad Formula with strong risk reversal
```

**One rule above all:** Match framework to funnel stage AND buyer awareness level. The best script
using the wrong framework for where the audience's head is will underperform a mediocre script
using the right one. Write the whole script to ONE awareness level — never try to target all
levels in a single ad.
